Assessment in this course establishes two different things: that a team can produce a working software system, and that each student can exercise engineering responsibility for the system they helped build. The artifact demonstrates the first; an individual oral examination demonstrates the second. Delegating work to an agent does not delegate responsibility, so the assessment deliberately separates the system produced from the engineering judgment and responsibility behind it.

Three complementary instruments:

- **[Project Assessment](project-assessment.md)** — the team's system is assessed through pods and cross-testing. Teams working from the same regulation converge on a shared specification, implement independently, and test one another's systems against it. The shared specification is the engineering artifact; cross-testing is both evidence for assessment and a feedback loop that surfaces specification gaps and conformance failures.
- **[Oral Exams](oral-exams.md)** — each student's ability to exercise engineering responsibility for the system is assessed directly. Two short, individually administered oral exams over the semester require students to explain the system, justify engineering decisions, reason about its behavior and limitations, and demonstrate that they can evaluate and control changes to it.
- **[Formative Assessment](formative-assessment.md)** — after each project phase, students reflect briefly on their developing engineering competencies and their experience in the course. The survey reminds students what they should be learning and gives course staff timely evidence about what is working, what is not, and where students need additional support.
