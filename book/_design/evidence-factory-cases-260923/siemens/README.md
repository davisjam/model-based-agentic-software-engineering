### A³E — the CAE research vision (DISW-Sim, [S1], 2026-07-03)

- **[E1]** "Unlike classical AI workflows – which automate a fixed sequence of steps predictably but inflexibly – an agentic AI system possesses autonomy." — [S1], 2026-07-03

- **[E3]** Architecture named as two layers: **Task Agents** (narrowly scoped, one workflow slice each — 3D simulation setup, reduced-order modeling, PLM integration, simulation recommendation) coordinated by **Agentic AI Orchestrators** that are "assembled by engineering experts." — [S1], 2026-07-03
- **[E4]** Models are retrieved *from* existing engineering representations: "a *PLM Interface Agent* can browse Teamcenter, retrieve a CAD model, and extract the relevant material specifications." — [S1], 2026-07-03

- **[E6]** Interoperability is protocol-mediated: the post names the **Model Context Protocol (MCP)** and the **Agent-to-Agent Protocol (A2A)**. — [S1], 2026-07-03
- **[E7]** "During this workflow a human engineer retains strategic oversight." — [S1], 2026-07-03
- **[E8]** **Status caveat, verbatim:** "The Foundry is a research vision and innovation roadmap, not a committed product delivery." and "This is a research exploration by the Simcenter Technology Innovation team… this blog represents only potential product innovations and does not constitute a commitment for delivery." — [S1], 2026-07-03
- **[E9]** **Open problems the authors themselves name:** "best practices for agentic patterns in low-error-tolerance engineering contexts, the right balance between agent autonomy and human-in-the-loop oversight, and how to capture engineering knowledge in ways that are simultaneously useful to an agent and auditable for compliance." — [S1], 2026-07-03

### Knowledge Fabric — Siemens changing its OWN software (CORP, [S8]/[S9])

- **[E20]** Knowledge Fabric is "an AI system for automating the software development lifecycle," built using "knowledge graphs on Spanner Graph, the Google Agent Development Kit, Gemini API, Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, Gemini CLI, and **Anthropic Claude Code**." — [S8], 2026-06-16
- **[E21]** **Scale:** "codebases spanning hundreds of millions of lines developed for over more than a decade." [S9] adds that this software supports "factories, energy grids and transportation networks." — [S8], 2026-06-16; [S9], 2026-06-23
- **[E22]** **The representation:** "We use Spanner Graph to model the inherent structure of the codebase, applying the same rigor to documentation across formats. By mapping connections between these domains, we can **link specific code snippets directly to requirements in a design document**. Agents then traverse this graph, using tools to query the structure via Graph Query Language (GQL)." Ingested material includes "code, Jira tickets, Confluence pages, and scanned PDF manuals from the early 2000s." Three retrieval modes: GQL structural traversal, embeddings + Approximate Nearest Neighbors, and full-text search combined with GQL. — [S8], 2026-06-16
- **[E23]** **The decomposition pattern:** "A key insight from the project was that AI agents struggle with massive, ambiguous tasks. To succeed, the team adopted a design pattern dubbed '**slicing the elephant**.'" - **[E24]** **The five named agents** (verbatim descriptions) — [S8], 2026-06-16:

  3. **Architecture impact agent** — "Analyzes proposed changes against the graph to predict side effects **before a single line of code is written**."
  4. **Task breakdown agent** — "Consumes the analysis from the architecture impact agent and breaks the work into small, manageable tasks, **each carrying all the context relevant to a specific change**."
  5. **Coding agent** — "Implements the change described in a specific task. **Reaching this step without context and prior analysis produces unusable code.**"

## § Claims

Organized on the §5.1 factory anatomy. Each claim carries its evidence and its
business unit.

### Where work originates

- **[C1] (SI-DI)** In the Eigen case, a change originates as a **task assigned against an existing engineering model** — the TIA Portal project — rather than against a repository or a ticket. The agent's first act is to read the automation model's structure, devices, and logic. [E15], [E12]
- **[C2] (CORP)** In Siemens' own software factory, a change originates as a **natural-language request from a product owner**, which a dedicated *user story agent* converts into "detailed user stories with acceptance criteria linked to existing system contexts" before any realization begins. Origination is therefore an agent-mediated interview, not a hand-authored ticket. [E24]
- **[C3] (DISW-Sim)** In the A³E vision, work originates in three distinct modes — "chat co-pilot interfaces, silent background processes triggered by design events, and embedded buttons in existing tools" — i.e. a design-model *event* can originate work with no human request at all. [E3], [S1]

### What humans do

- **[C4] (all units)** Across every Siemens source, the human retains **strategic scoping and the consequential decision**, and the descriptions are consistent about it — "a human engineer retains strategic oversight" [E7]; "will wait for Sarah's feedback before proceeding" [E37]; "configurable human oversight at critical decision points" [E16]; "human in the loop at every step" [E25]; "with human oversight… people still in control" [E33].
- **[C5] (DISW-Sim)** A³E makes the human's *upstream* role structural by naming three roles — **Template Developers** who build agents, **Workflow Creators** who configure orchestrations, **End Users** who run pre-configured workflows — so most humans consume an environment that other humans engineered. This is the book's "judgment moves upstream" pattern stated as an org chart. [E3], [S1]
- **[C6] (CORP)** Siemens' own factory reports the *reason* for the human-in-the-loop posture as a property obligation, not a maturity concession: industrial software must be "explainable, traceable, and verifiable" across "15 to 20 years of operation," and "hallucinated or unvalidated changes are not merely inefficient but operationally unacceptable." [E26]

### What agents do

- **[C7] (SI-DI)** Eigen performs **software realization in a narrow, model-grounded language**: it generates and adapts SCL and LAD PLC code, generates test logic, fixes compile and syntax errors, generates HMI JavaScript, configures devices and PROFINET, and runs style-guide checks. This is the one Siemens case where an agent's primary output is production control *software* derived from an engineering model. [E15a], [E12]
- **[C8] (CORP)** Siemens decomposes agent work into **five role-typed agents with an enforced ordering**, and states the failure mode of skipping the ordering in one sentence: the coding agent "implements the change described in a specific task. Reaching this step without context and prior analysis produces unusable code." The agent's *share* of realization is large; its *autonomy over scope* is deliberately small. [E24], [E23]
- **[C9] (SEDA)** In EDA, agents do the labor of verification — testbench generation, debug, property generation from "plain English specifications into SystemVerilog assertions" — a task whose human apprenticeship is stated at "six to nine months to become proficient." The delegation target is expertise scarcity, matching A³E's "for every one engineer who can confidently run a simulation, there are roughly ten who urgently need one but cannot." [E16c], [E9a]

### What the fabricator inherits

- **[C10] (CORP)** The inherited context is an explicit **typed graph of the software ecosystem** — code structure ("a class belongs to a file, which belongs to a module"), Jira, Confluence, and scanned PDF manuals from the early 2000s — with the load-bearing edge being that it can "link specific code snippets directly to requirements in a design document," queried three ways (GQL, ANN embeddings, full-text). This is a *knowledge* representation in the book's sense, not a behavioral model. [E22]
- **[C11] (SI-DI)** Eigen inherits the **engineering model itself** — the TIA Portal project — as its context, which is categorically different from inheriting a repository. The automation model already encodes devices, network topology, and control logic; the agent does not have to recover them from source. [E15]
- **[C12] (DISW-Sim)** A³E's agents inherit both CAD/material data pulled from Teamcenter and "a company's historical simulation knowledge" held in a knowledge graph, exchanged between agents through **standardized artifacts** (FMU) and **standardized protocols** (MCP, A2A). Siemens is explicitly betting on artifact and protocol standardization as the interoperability layer between agents. [E4], [E5], [E6]
- **[C13] (DISW-PLM)** Intelligence Center X generalizes the inheritance claim to the enterprise: "industrial ontologies" plus a knowledge graph on "one governed foundation." The promise is context-as-a-platform. [E32]

### What the agent may decide for itself

- **[C14] (SI-DI) — the sharpest evidence in this file for the book's thesis.** Eigen's admission criterion is stated as a **pre-set acceptance region, not a specified realization**: "It understands its projects, writes automation code, configures systems, and **iterates until pre-defined performance benchmarks are met**." The benchmark is fixed upstream; everything about *how* the code reaches it is the agent's to decide. That is a tolerance in §5.1's exact sense — state the acceptable region, allow fabrication freedom inside it — and it is the cleanest public instance of a model-first factory that does **not** determine realization. [E12]
- **[C15] (SEDA)** Fuse and Questa One bound agent freedom by **capability**, not by specification: role-based access controls, audit trails, human checkpoints, domain-specific guardrails, air-gapped execution, "customer-defined governance boundaries." Siemens' answer to "what may an agent decide" is a permission surface plus a validator, not a more complete model. [E17], [E16]
- **[C16] (CORP)** Siemens' own factory narrows agent decision scope *by construction*: work is sliced into tasks "each carrying all the context relevant to a specific change," because "AI agents struggle with massive, ambiguous tasks." Realization freedom is managed through **task size** rather than through specification density. [E23], [E24]

### Where quality is established

- **[C17] (SEDA) — the strongest mechanism claim.** Siemens EDA's stated architecture places the evidence source **outside the model**: agents "continuously validate their decisions against proven engineering tools," specifically "physics-based EDA engines," and validate "against golden test harness" — the pairing Siemens markets as "**self-verifying AI workflows**." NVIDIA's counterpart states the same split: "AI agents need trusted tools to reason, act and verify their work." Quality is established by a deterministic engine the agent can call in a loop, not by human reading and not by the model's completeness. [E18], [E18a]
- **[C18] (DISW-Sim)** Agent Studio states the same principle two years earlier and in the systems-engineering domain: "Unlike traditional LLM chatbots that exclusively use the LLM's knowledge, multi-agent systems make use of rigorous tools that validate the results," with Simcenter Amesim simulation as the validator of generated EV architectures. Siemens has held this position since at least **2024-12-12**. [E37]
- **[C19] (CORP)** By contrast, in Siemens' *own* software factory the public account names **no automated gate at all** — no test suite, no CI, no merge gate, no coverage threshold. The described quality mechanism is human review at every step, plus one preventive analysis step (the architecture impact agent predicting side effects "before a single line of code is written"). Where Siemens sells engineering software it describes deterministic validators; where it builds its own software it describes human review. [E25], [E30], [E24]
- **[C20] (SEDA)** Quality is also established **earlier in the flow** than the artifact: the architecture impact agent [C19] and the formal-property generation path [E16c] both move evidence upstream of implementation, which is the §5.1 move of detaching supervision from the fabricated artifact.

### Who or what holds admission authority

- **[C21] (SI-DI)** In the Eigen account, admission authority appears to be **machine-held and threshold-shaped** — "pre-defined performance benchmarks" — and the press release names no human approval step, no review workflow, and no governance mechanism whatsoever. This silence is itself a strong finding: the most autonomous Siemens product is also the least explicit about who says yes. [E12], [E13], [E15c]
- **[C22] (SEDA)** In EDA, admission is **mixed**: machine-held for the continuously-run validators, human-held at named checkpoints, with "RTL sign-off" as the declared goal state. "Configurable human oversight at critical decision points" makes the boundary itself a customer-set parameter — admission authority is configuration, not architecture. [E16], [E16a], [E17]
- **[C23] (CORP)** In Siemens' own factory, admission is **entirely human**: "a human in the loop at every step." The account grounds the human gate in the compliance obligation [E26] and nowhere promises to automate approval; whether Siemens regards the human gate as permanent is **not publicly stated** — permanence is an inference the record does not license. [E25], [E26]
- **[C24] (DISW-PLM)** The Teamcenter BOM agent's authority is explicitly bounded to **propose-and-act-under-oversight** — "propose changes and act on them… with human oversight" — but the source does not say which of those two verbs is gated. [E33], [E34]

### How experience changes the factory

- **[C25] (SEDA)** Questa One claims "**persistent expertise building**" and "adaptive cross-run strategies" — an explicit claim that the factory learns across runs — but [S4] does not explain the mechanism, and no other Siemens source substantiates it. Record as claimed-but-unmechanized. [E16], [E16b]
- **[C26] (DISW-PLM)** Siemens' own definition of an agent includes a **Learn** step ("the AI agent can be finetuned to keep learning"), locating experience in model weights rather than in externalized artifacts — the opposite of the skills/instructions pattern the software-first cases show. [E38]
- **[C27] (CORP)** In Knowledge Fabric, the persisting asset is the **graph**, not the agent: the representation accumulates, and each new task queries it. But the account says nothing about whether a completed change writes anything back — no learned constraint, no updated instruction, no reusable procedure. [E22], [E31]

### Cross-cutting claims the book may want

- **[C28]** **Siemens is mostly a factory-in-a-box vendor, not a published internal factory.** Eleven of the fourteen sources describe production systems Siemens *sells* to other engineering organizations; exactly one ([S8], with its derivative [S9]) describes Siemens changing its own software, and that one was written by the cloud vendor rather than by Siemens. Any book claim about "the Siemens factory" must say which. [S1]–[S14]
- **[C29]** **The book's current `siemens2026a3e` cite is weaker than the book's use of it.** [S1] is (a) about **simulation/CAE**, containing **no sentences about code generation or software** [E9a]; (b) explicitly "a research vision and innovation roadmap, not a committed product delivery" [E8]; and (c) authored by an innovation team whose own boilerplate disclaims delivery commitment. The bib entry also carries only the blog-root URL `https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/`, not the article URL — it should be repointed to the [S1] URL with date 2026-07-03. The load-bearing model-first-software claim is better carried by Eigen [E12]/[E15a] (SI-DI) and Agent Studio [E37] (DISW-Sim), with A³E as supporting architecture.
- **[C30]** **The A³E authors themselves name the book's open question.** They list as unsolved "the right balance between agent autonomy and human-in-the-loop oversight, and how to capture engineering knowledge in ways that are simultaneously useful to an agent and **auditable for compliance**." A practitioner source stating the problem in the book's own terms is stronger evidence for the question's reality than for any answer. [E9]
- **[C31]** **Siemens' position is "agent plus deterministic engine," not "model determines code."** Across SEDA [E18], DISW-Sim [E37], and SI-DI [E12], the recurring structure is: a model or spec supplies the *goal and the context*, a deterministic engine (physics solver, simulation, compiler, golden harness, benchmark) supplies the *verdict*, and the agent freely searches between them. This is precisely the bargain §5.1 says MDD could not strike, and Siemens is the clearest public instance of an organization striking it — in engineering domains, not yet in its own software.

## [GAPS] — what the public record does NOT answer for Siemens

- **G1. No model↔implementation conformance gate is established anywhere.** A targeted search found **no Siemens source** describing a mechanical check that compares a declared model relation to an implementation and **rejects** a change when the relation no longer holds. The closest are Eigen's "pre-defined performance benchmarks" [E12] (a threshold, not a correspondence) and the EDA golden test harness [E18] (a reference behavior, not a model-to-code relation). **The book's existing statement of this gap is correct and should be retained.**
- **G2. Which business unit owns Knowledge Fabric is not stated.** Neither [S8] nor [S9] names the division; [S9] says so explicitly [E30]. Franz Menzl's title ("SVP, product creation excellence") and the pilot workload ("legacy control panels") suggest industrial software, but the record does not establish it and this file does not infer it.
- **G3. No quantity for Siemens' own software factory.** Every benefit in [S8]/[S9] is unquantified — "far less time," "reduced overall coding effort" [E27]. No percentage, no hours, no throughput, no defect rate, no adoption count, no headcount. Contrast [E10]/[E11]/[E19], where the *products* carry numbers.
- **G4. No automated admission gate is described for Siemens' own software.** No test suite, CI, merge gate, static analysis, coverage threshold, or validator appears in [S8]/[S9]. Whether "human in the loop at every step" [E25] sits *alongside* or *instead of* mechanical gates is not established.
- **G5. Eigen's governance surface is entirely absent from the public record.** Neither [S2] nor [S3] describes any human review, approval, simulation-before-deploy, safety analysis, or guardrail [E13], [E15c]. For an agent that writes PLC code destined for physical machinery, the silence on who authorizes deployment is the single most conspicuous gap in this file.
- **G6. "Customer-defined governance boundaries" is undefined.** [S4] introduces the phrase and never says what a boundary can express, how it is authored, whether it is machine-enforced, or what happens on violation [E16b]. Likewise "persistent expertise building" [C25] and "production-grade governance… repeatability" [E19b] — vocabulary without mechanism.
- **G7. How experience changes any Siemens factory is unestablished.** [E38]'s "finetuned" is a definition, not a report; [E16]'s "persistent expertise building" has no mechanism; [E31] records that Knowledge Fabric says nothing about write-back. No Siemens source describes a learned constraint, a shared instruction file, a skill, or a reusable procedure accumulating from completed work.
- **G8. Siemens publishes no first-party engineering account of its own agentic software development.** The only account is vendor-authored [S8] and its derivative [S9]. There is no Siemens engineering blog post, conference talk, or paper describing how Siemens engineers work with coding agents day to day.
- **G9. Model-first for *software* is thinner than model-first for *engineering*.** [S1] — the book's current cite — is about simulation and contains no software-realization content [E9a]. [S12] generates architectures, not code. Only Eigen [E15a] shows software fabricated downstream of a model, and it is a narrow domain (IEC-61131 control logic in a single vendor's IDE). The record does **not** establish that Siemens runs a model-first factory for general-purpose software.
- **G10. Nothing about the economics.** No Siemens source states agent cost, token spend, retry rates, failure rates, rework, or the cost of maintaining the models and graphs the agents consume. [E19]'s "5X to 10X reduction in token costs" is the sole cost figure anywhere in the corpus, and it is about EDA characterization, not software change.
- **G11. Nothing about what happens when an agent is wrong.** No Siemens source describes rollback, incident response, post-mortem, attribution of a defect to an agent, or a case where an agent's change was rejected. [E26] states the stakes ("operationally unacceptable") without describing the response.
- **G12. Cross-unit integration is asserted, not shown.** Intelligence Center X claims to unify agents across engineering, manufacturing, supply chain, and service on "one governed foundation" [E32], but no source shows an agent crossing a unit boundary — e.g. a Teamcenter BOM change propagating to a TIA Portal PLC change. The units' agent stories remain separate in the public record.
- **G13. ARC Advisory's "context layer / agentic foundry" analysis is unread** (HTTP 403, 2026-09-23). If it matters to the argument, it needs a different access route.
- **G14 — The moment and actor of Eigen's admission.** [E12] states the acceptance criterion ("iterates until pre-defined performance benchmarks are met") but who or what accepts the iterated result into the deployed automation project — a person, a deployment gate, or nothing beyond the benchmark itself — is **not publicly stated** ([E13], [E15c] record the targeted reads that found no such sentence).
- **G15 — Authorship and auditability of Eigen's benchmarks.** The admission criterion is "pre-defined performance benchmarks" [E12], but who defines them — Siemens, the customer, or a product default — and whether they are inspectable, versioned, or auditable artifacts is not publicly stated. For the record's only machine-held, threshold-shaped admission criterion, both the author of the threshold and the reviewability of the threshold are absent from the public account.
- **G16 — Control lifetime is unaddressed in every unit.** "Configurable human oversight at critical decision points" [E16] and Fuse's "human checkpoints" [E17] are described as present-tense features; whether any checkpoint, guardrail, or governance boundary is expected to relax, strengthen, or retire as agent capability changes is not publicly stated in any Siemens source.
- **G17 — How the Knowledge Fabric graph is kept true of the codebase is not publicly stated.** [E22] describes ingestion of code, Jira, Confluence, and scanned manuals into Spanner Graph; no source states whether ingestion is continuous or one-time, how the graph is updated when the code changes, or whether code-to-requirement links are ever re-validated. For a system whose stated obligation is outputs that are "explainable, traceable, and verifiable" over 15–20 years of operation [E26], the maintenance of the traceability substrate is the record's least-supported link.
