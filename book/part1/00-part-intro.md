When a constraint moves, engineering reorganizes [cite: amdahl1967;goldratt1984]. Steam engines made mechanical power widely available, shifting engineering effort toward designing machinery that could harness and transmit that power. Mechanized factories made production faster and cheaper, shifting engineering effort toward designing products for manufacture and organizing the machinery and processes that produced them. Coding agents are now reducing the cost of software implementation. I argue that their capabilities warrant a similar reorganization of engineering work. Engineers can delegate more implementation decisions while retaining responsibility for the resulting systems. The challenge is to exercise informed control without inspecting or directing every implementation decision.

We begin with an analogy for coding agents in [ref:chapter:1.1-the-printer], then examine the engineering decisions that remain when implementation is delegated in [ref:chapter:1.2-the-engineering-object]. [ref:chapter:1.3-mage-by-example] introduces DocAble, the book’s principal worked example. [ref:chapter:1.4-the-agentic-substrate] examines the capabilities and limitations of contemporary agents, and [ref:chapter:1.5-the-new-engineering-problem] identifies the two engineering problems that motivate MAGE.

> ### Key Ideas {#founding-premise}
>
> Engineering effort concentrates around what limits reliable production. Commodity intelligence makes implementation abundant relative to engineering judgment. Engineers can delegate more realization work, but remain responsible for retaining sufficient control over the resulting system.
