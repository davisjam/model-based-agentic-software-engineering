# MAGE by Example

<!-- point: teaser-grounds-the-abstractions | You will meet DocAble in every chapter; here is just enough to hold it. | terms: running-example -->
The last chapter closed on a promise: the running example of this book is a real, shipped system.
You will meet it in every chapter that follows, so it helps to hold a little of it in your head now —
not the architecture, and not the story of how it was built, both of which wait for
[The Built System](5.4-the-built-system.html) and {{part:5}}. Just enough to know what kind of object
needed all this machinery, and why it exists at all.

## Two birds, one stone

<!-- section-terms: document-accessibility, supervised-autonomy -->

<!-- point: explicit-problem-inaccessible-documents-at-scale | The explicit problem: inaccessible university documents at scale. | terms: document-accessibility, remediation-cost -->
The explicit problem is a mountain of inaccessible documents. In 2024 the Department of
Justice gave public universities a deadline: the slide decks, PDFs, and spreadsheets they
distribute must meet a real accessibility standard, so that a student using a screen reader
gets the same course as everyone else. A typical deck fails that standard dozens of ways —
images with no descriptions, missing titles, a reading order no machine can follow.
Remediating one course's materials by hand costs roughly {{workweeks_per_course}} full work
weeks, and a university offers thousands of courses. The mandate is just; the arithmetic is
crushing. [The ADA Context](5.1-the-ada-context.html) walks the full bind.

<!-- point: two-birds-one-stone-motive | One project answered both problems at once. | terms: document-accessibility, running-example -->
The implicit problem was mine. In early 2026 coding agents got good — good enough that I, a
professor of software engineering, no longer knew what my discipline's daily craft was about to
become, or what I should be teaching in the fall. The only way to find out was to build something
real with the agents and watch what the work demanded. The two problems met in a committee meeting
about that very deadline. Bored, I opened a chat model, fed it screenshots from one of my own decks,
and asked it to transcribe what it saw. It could. That five-minute experiment said the technical
core was suddenly within reach, and one project could answer both problems at once: build the
remediation system, and let the building teach the method. Two birds, one stone. The system is
[DocAble]({{product_url}}), live in beta; the method is MAGE, and this book is the field report from
watching it emerge.

## What the user sees

<!-- section-terms: governance-target-product, document-accessibility -->

<!-- point: upload-in-remediated-file-plus-evidence-out | A user uploads a document; a checked file and its evidence return. | terms: governance-target-product -->
From the outside the system is almost boring, by design. A professor drops a slide deck onto a web
page; a few minutes later a remediated version comes back — figures described, slides titled, reading
order set, contrast fixed — together with a record of every change and the clause of the standard each
remaining finding answers to. The evidence travels with the file, because a university needs not just
fixed documents but proof of what was fixed, and the ability to reverse any change that got the meaning
wrong. [ref:docable-glimpse] shows only that: what goes in, and what comes back.

## Where the map leads

<!-- section-terms: thesis-modeling, thesis-alignment -->

<!-- point: parts-2-4-machinery-part-5-full-case | Parts 2–4 explain the machinery; Part 5 the full case. | terms: thesis-modeling, thesis-alignment -->
That is enough of the object. The rest of the book explains the machinery that produces it — the
models in Part 2, the governed environment in Part 3, both as daily practice in Part 4 — and
{{part:5}} reconstructs the full case: the six-part pipeline inside the machine, the fleet of agents
that wrote it, the failures that taught the method, and the road from the committee meeting where it
started to the system that shipped. Part 5 is not offered as proof — one system, one engineer, one
model vendor is a case, not a law. It is the grounding case: the system this method was derived from,
and the place every claim in the coming chapters can be traced back to.
