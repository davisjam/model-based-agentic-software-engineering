# Two Problems the Method Must Solve

<!-- point: substrate-leaves-two-problems | Two problems remain: representation, and authority outside the producing run. | terms: engineering-substrate, foundation-model, agentic-harness -->
The preceding substrate properties leave two engineering problems that MAGE addresses directly.
A finite reasoner needs representations that keep relevant state tractable as systems and tasks
grow. Probabilistic autonomous work needs authority outside the producing run so that important
obligations do not depend on memory, confidence, or repeated human inspection.

Put differently, commodity intelligence exposes two related limits. A finite reasoner cannot keep
arbitrarily much consequential state usefully active, and a fallible reasoner cannot by inference alone
guarantee every consequential judgment it makes. The first is a problem of reasoning reach; the second is a
problem of assurance. MAGE addresses them differently: Part II changes the representation over which
reasoning occurs, and Part III gives selected obligations authority independent of the producing run.

## The Representation Problem

<!-- point: part-one-poses-the-representation-problem | The representation problem: how should a large evolving system be represented so a finite reasoner works coherently over the properties that matter without reconstructing them from implementation? | terms: engineering-substrate, reasoning-horizon, modeling-principle -->
A broad reasoner can operate on abstractions, but its active reasoning state is finite and
large systems contain far more detail than any one engineering question needs. **How should a
large, evolving system be represented so that a finite reasoner can work coherently over the
properties that matter without continually reconstructing them from implementation?**

Part II develops MAGE's answer: Modeling.

## The Authority Problem

<!-- point: part-one-poses-the-authority-problem | The authority problem: autonomous work can be locally constrained and checked without a comprehensive model, but probabilistic implementation cannot be its own final authority, and richer obligations need semantics the environment must obtain somewhere. | terms: engineering-substrate, alignment-principle, injection-point -->
Autonomous work can be locally constrained and checked without a comprehensive model. But
probabilistic implementation cannot be its own final authority, and richer system-level
obligations require the environment to obtain their meaning from somewhere. **How should engineering
obligations be given authority over autonomous work so that satisfying them does not depend on the
producing agent remembering, agreeing, or self-certifying—and so that deterministically
checkable questions do not require a human to reconstruct the answer each time?**

Part III develops MAGE's answer: Alignment.

## How Representation and Authority Relate

<!-- point: representation-and-authority-related-not-the-same | The two problems are related but neither reducible to the other; the book develops Modeling first, not because Alignment is impossible without it — local controls can already be authoritative, but better abstractions enlarge what can be stated, reasoned over, and checked without recurring human reconstruction. Commodity intelligence reduces implementation work, not the decisions. | terms: engineering-substrate, modeling-principle, alignment-principle -->
The two problems are related but neither is reducible to the other. Representation shapes
the scale and semantics of what humans, agents, and tools can reason over; authority determines
what the resulting work must satisfy before it is accepted. Local controls can already be authoritative
without rich models. Better abstractions, however, enlarge the set of consequential properties that can be
stated, reasoned about, and checked without recurring human reconstruction. Part II develops the
abstractions; Part III shows how obligations acquire authority; Part IV combines the two into the method.
[ref:substrate-to-method] traces that derivation. Commodity intelligence reduces the implementation work
required to realize many engineering decisions; it does not remove the decisions themselves.
