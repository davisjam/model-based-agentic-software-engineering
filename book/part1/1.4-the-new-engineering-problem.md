# Two Problems the Method Must Solve

<!-- point: substrate-leaves-two-problems | The properties of agentic machinery leave two engineering problems: a finite reasoner needs representations that keep relevant state tractable, and fallible autonomous work needs enforcement outside the producing run. | terms: engineering-substrate, foundation-model, agentic-harness -->
The properties of agentic machinery leave two engineering problems. A finite reasoner needs
representations that keep relevant system state tractable as systems and tasks grow. Fallible
autonomous work needs enforcement outside the producing run so that important obligations do not depend on
the producer resolving every consequential judgment correctly.

<!-- point: the-two-problems-representation-and-enforcement | The two problems, related but distinct: the Representation Problem (how to represent a large system so relevant properties are reasoned about without reconstruction) answered by Modeling in Part II, and the Enforcement Problem (how intent comes to bind autonomous work so satisfying obligations does not depend on the producer self-certifying) answered by Alignment in Part III. | terms: engineering-substrate, modeling-principle, alignment-principle, reasoning-horizon, injection-point -->
These problems are related but distinct:

1. **The Representation Problem.** Large systems contain far more detail than any one engineering question
   needs, while the active state available to a reasoner is finite. How should a large, evolving system be
   represented so that the properties relevant to an engineering question can be reasoned about without
   continually reconstructing them from implementation? Part II develops MAGE's answer: Modeling.
2. **The Enforcement Problem.** Autonomous work can be constrained and checked at the interfaces through which
   it acts, but the producing run cannot be the final check on its own consequential judgments. How
   should engineering intent be enforced over autonomous work, so that satisfying important
   obligations does not depend on the producing agent remembering, agreeing, or self-certifying each time?
   Part III develops MAGE's answer: Alignment.

<!-- point: modeling-and-alignment-complement-one-another | Modeling makes selected properties explicit enough to reason about; Alignment enforces selected obligations over realization; neither requires the other in every case, but together they let engineering knowledge and judgment persist outside any one producing run. | terms: modeling-principle, alignment-principle -->
The two activities complement one another. Modeling makes selected system properties explicit enough to
reason about; Alignment enforces selected obligations over realization. Neither requires the other
in every case: useful representations need not become constraints, and some local controls can act without
a rich system model. Together, however, they let engineering knowledge and judgment persist outside any one
producing run. [ref:substrate-to-method] traces that derivation.
