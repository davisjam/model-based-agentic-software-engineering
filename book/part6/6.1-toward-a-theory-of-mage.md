# A Theory of MAGE

Parts II–V built the method from engineering practice. This chapter compresses that account into a
theory: what the important constructs are, how they interact, where the mechanism should fail, and what
observations would count against it. The theory is deliberately directional rather than numerical. One
deeply observed system can expose mechanisms and sequence; it cannot supply universal
coefficients.[^theory-kind]

[^theory-kind]: In theory-building terms, MAGE is a *middle-range theory*: an account of mechanisms and scope conditions for a bounded class of phenomena rather than a claim to universal law.

The central claim is simple. **Agentic capacity amplifies the engineering environment in which it
operates.** A strong environment turns more implementation capacity into durable progress. A weak or
incoherent one turns the same capacity into churn, escaped defects, repeated intervention, and control
friction. Those failures can reveal what the environment is missing; engineering judgment determines
whether the problem is local or structural; and adaptation changes the representations, mechanisms, or
architecture inherited by later work. As the environment improves, larger tasks and greater autonomy
become feasible—and expose the next frontier.

> ### Theory of MAGE — informal
>
> Agentic capacity does not by itself produce engineering progress. It magnifies the fit—or misfit—between
> the work attempted and the engineering environment that surrounds it.

## The dynamic model

[ref:mage-dynamic-model] puts the theory on one diagram. **Agentic capacity** supplies model capability,
concurrency, and raw implementation rate. The **governed engineering environment** supplies the
representations, constraints, evidence, and authority through which that capacity acts. Together they
produce **realized performance**: durable throughput, defect escape, and the human attention consumed per
unit of useful output.

The difference between apparent capacity and durable performance registers as **governance
pressure**—churn, recurring failure, escaped defects, repeated intervention, or friction introduced by
the governance itself. Pressure is evidence, not diagnosis. An engineer or organization still has to
decide whether the signal is a local defect, a missing representation, a weak boundary, an inadequate
control, or an obsolete piece of machinery. That diagnosis drives adaptation of the environment, and the
changed environment shapes the next round of work.

Four dynamics run over that spine. **Bootstrap** carries prior engineering knowledge into the environment
before work begins. **Discovery** exposes mismatches between the work attempted and the environment
available to support it. **Adaptation** changes the environment in response. **Capability amplification**
follows successful adaptation: stronger environments support larger tasks, greater concurrency, or more
ambitious requirements, which push work onto a new frontier. MAGE therefore has no reason to converge
while capability, ambition, or the system itself keeps changing.

Not all useful structure is discovered through failure. Existing architecture, standards, requirements,
operating history, and lessons imported from earlier systems can seed the environment before an agent
acts. Other needs become visible only in use. MAGE therefore has two routes into durable structure: **ex
ante**, where a known obligation or useful representation is engineered deliberately, and **ex post**,
where experience exposes a recurring cost or risk worth externalizing. Governance conversion[^provenance]
names the latter route; it is not the origin story of every model or mechanism.

[^provenance]: The earlier case-study paper [cite: davis2026cheapcode] induced governance conversion from the first twelve weeks of DocAble. Continued work added ex-ante structure, Modeling as a coequal engineering activity, non-monotonic accumulation, and the broader environmental dynamics developed here.

Two conditions decide whether the loop turns, and each sits on one transition. Reading pressure as
structural rather than local is a scarce diagnostic capability; it sits on the step from pressure to
diagnosis. Acting on that diagnosis requires authority over the shared environment—the architecture, the
models, the mechanisms—and sits one step later. Where either is missing the loop stalls: pressure with no
diagnosis is motion without direction, and a diagnosis with no authority dies as a local patch while its
failure class survives. The next chapter develops both as scope conditions, alongside how far a domain's
important properties can be governed at all.

## Effective environment quality

The theory cannot use apparatus quantity as a proxy for environment quality. Ten thousand lines of stale
checks are not better than one hundred lines that cover the obligations that matter. Call the relevant
construct **effective governed-environment quality**: the degree to which the environment gives a reasoner
useful, current representations of the system and proportionate coverage of the obligations relevant to
the work.

Four questions make the definition concrete. **Representation:** are the properties needed for the work
explicit at an appropriate abstraction? **Coverage:** are important obligations supplied with adequate
evidence and authority? **Coherence:** do the representations and mechanisms compose without contradiction
or harmful duplication? **Economy:** does the future value they produce justify their friction,
maintenance, and context cost? These are dimensions, not a scalar score. A larger environment can improve
any of them or make all four worse.

Coverage is not hardness. Some obligations belong in a compiler rule or admission gate; others are
statistical, semantic, or consequential enough that human judgment remains the appropriate authority. A
strong environment does not mechanize everything. It makes explicit **where judgment belongs and where
repeatedly paying for it would be wasteful**.

This makes MAGE a repertoire of capabilities rather than a maturity ladder. A project does not become
"more MAGE" by accumulating mechanisms or climbing prescribed levels. Its next investment follows the
bottleneck: strengthen representation where reconstruction dominates; strengthen evidence or authority
where failures escape; retire machinery where governance itself has become the tax. Dependencies can
create a local order of adoption, but dependency is not maturity.

## Outcomes and observables

A theory of agentic engineering should distinguish activity from progress. MAGE therefore treats raw
implementation velocity as an input and watches three outcomes: **durable throughput**, **defect escape**,
and **human-attention burden**. [ref:mage-outcomes] states each with the observables that would track it.

<!-- label: mage-outcomes -->
<!-- table: *Outcomes of the theory.* Raw implementation activity is an input. The theory is interested in how much survives as durable progress, what failures escape, and how much scarce human judgment that progress consumes. [short: The three MAGE outcomes and their observables] -->
| Outcome | What it measures | Example observables |
|---|---|---|
| **Durable throughput** | Raw implementation converted into lasting system progress | landed changes net of rework; churn share; the slope of sustained throughput |
| **Defect escape** | Relevant failures reaching later lifecycle stages or production | escaped-defect rate; reopen rate; silent-corruption escape |
| **Human-attention burden** | Human judgment required per unit of durable output | interventions per landed change; review time; conversion effort |

Durable throughput is useful system progress that survives rework, integration, and operation rather than
merely landing in the repository. Defect escape records consequential failures that reach later lifecycle
stages or production. Human-attention burden records the scarce engineering judgment consumed per unit of
durable progress: review, intervention, diagnosis, adjudication, and other work the environment cannot
carry itself.

These outcomes matter because the easiest quantities to count measure something else. Commits, lines
changed, control files, model-sync events, and support ratio measure activity or investment. They can help
explain what an environment is doing, but none establishes that the environment is producing a
return.[^activity-data] A large control estate may be capital, bureaucracy, or some mixture of both.

[^activity-data]: The activity-versus-outcome gap is already visible in industry data. A 2026 benchmark of 83,000 developers across 253 organizations [cite: linearb2026] reports large merge-rate gains among heavy AI users, then shows why merge rate alone misleads: among the heaviest users, yield fell even as merge rates roughly doubled. As one industry analyst put it, rising AI spend has not produced proportionate shipping velocity, and the difficulty has moved from comprehension to confidence—teams understand and change code more easily, but trust less what they release [cite: riggins2026]. The question is not whether AI increases activity, but whether that activity survives review, rework, integration, and operation as durable progress.

## What the theory predicts

The dynamic model makes three predictions load-bearing enough to belong in the statement of the theory
itself.

**Environment fit moderates velocity.** Increasing agentic capacity should produce more durable throughput
when the relevant engineering environment is strong and more churn, escaped failure, or repeated
intervention when it is weak. The prediction is not that "more governance is better." It is that the effect
of velocity depends on whether the environment supplies the representations, evidence, authority, and
coherence the attempted work requires.

**Representation creates leverage.** A task-relevant, trustworthy model should reduce the amount of
lower-level state a human or agent must reconstruct to answer the engineering question at hand. The
advantage should matter most as the required reasoning state grows. The claim fails if models add
maintenance and interpretation cost without extending reasoning reach or preserving quality.

**Engineering capital amortizes judgment.** Where useful engineering knowledge or recurring judgment
becomes durable structure, later work over the covered surface should require less reconstruction, repeated
decision-making, rework, or risk. The prediction includes its own boundary: the return lasts only while the
asset remains fit. Stale models, obsolete validators, conflicting controls, and procedures that outlive
their problem should lose value and can eventually impose net cost.

Those statements are deliberately falsifiable. Velocity may turn out to have roughly the same relationship
to durable progress regardless of environment quality. Explicit models may prove no better than
well-selected implementation context. The maintenance burden of accumulated engineering structure may
consume the savings it was supposed to create. Any of those results would require revision of the theory
rather than another mechanism added to save it.

The research chapter later in this Part expands these into eight named hypotheses across three
families—trustworthy representations, adaptive governance, and engineering economics—each with the study
designs that could confirm, narrow, or overturn it.

### The reasoning-horizon proposition

The representation-leverage prediction rests on a claim about scale the book has built toward since Part I.

> **Reasoning-Horizon Proposition.** Large software systems require more working state than any finite
> reasoner can keep active at once. Task-relevant models can extend the effective reasoning horizon by
> replacing implementation detail with semantically richer representations of the properties under
> consideration. The gain holds only while the representation is relevant, sufficiently faithful to the
> relation it claims, and cheaper to use than reconstructing the same knowledge from lower-level artifacts.

Agentic systems do not create this scale problem. Human engineers have always reasoned about large systems
through abstractions because nobody can hold the implementation of a Linux-scale system in active view.
Commodity intelligence changes the economics and frequency of the problem: larger delegated tasks and
greater implementation volume increase the return on representations that keep relevant state available
across reasoning episodes.

### The engineering-capital proposition

The amortization prediction rests on a claim about what durable structure is worth.

> **Engineering-Capital Proposition.** Durable engineering structures constitute capital when future work
> inherits useful capacity from them: less reconstruction, less repeated judgment, earlier or stronger
> evidence, safer action, or cheaper recovery. Models, mechanisms, architectures, procedures, and other
> engineering assets can all qualify. Their returns are local to the surfaces they help and last only while
> the assets remain fit.

The analogy to technical debt is intentional. Debt makes future change more expensive; capital makes
future change more productive. Neither status is permanent. Capital depreciates as systems, obligations,
and tools change. A validator can become noise, a model can drift, and a once-useful constraint can begin
blocking legitimate work. Mature governance therefore includes maintenance, reconciliation, and
retirement. **Accumulation is not the objective; productive capacity is.**

## Modeling expands the surface of Alignment

Modeling and Alignment are distinct engineering moves. **Modeling changes what a reasoner can see;
Alignment changes what the environment allows, requires, or admits.** Neither logically requires the other.
A type can rule out an invalid state, a sandbox can deny a capability, a permission boundary can reject an
action, and a test can block a regression without a rich explicit model of the surrounding system.

Modeling matters because it changes the scale and kind of properties that can become tractable. Suppose the
architecture requires every permitted route to a remediation service to cross quota authorization.
Reasoning from implementation requires reconstructing routes from handlers, middleware, configuration, and
dependency injection. Represent the permitted routes explicitly and the same architectural obligation
becomes a graph question: does every allowed path cross the quota boundary? The property did not become
less semantic. **The representation changed the object over which the question is decided.**

This gives the two theses their mature relationship. Alignment can operate directly over actions and
artifacts. Modeling exposes additional system-level properties over which Alignment can operate. A model
can also earn value without binding anything: if it saves later engineers and agents from repeatedly
reconstructing the same system knowledge, it is already producing engineering capital. Authority adds
another return when a stable obligation over that representation becomes suitable for environmental
enforcement.

**Alignment does not require Modeling. Modeling enlarges and strengthens the surface over which Alignment
can operate.**

## What the evidence supports

MAGE currently rests on **two kinds of evidence with complementary strengths**.

The originating case supplies **mechanism depth**. DocAble was observed longitudinally from inside the
engineering process, with repository history, field notes, incidents, models, controls, and operational
evidence available to reconstruct sequence. That evidence can support claims such as: this problem
occurred; this engineering response followed; the response altered the environment in this way; and later
work did or did not expose the same class again. It is strongest for explaining **how particular structures
emerged inside one system**. It cannot establish how common those mechanisms are, how large their effects
would be elsewhere, or how DocAble would have performed under a matched alternative process.

The industrial reconstructions supply **variation**. Cloudflare, Docker, Siemens, Shopify, Spotify, and
Zenseact developed their systems independently and under materially different constraints. Their public
accounts can show that comparable engineering structures arise outside the originating case, demonstrate
alternative operationalizations, and expose boundaries that DocAble could not. They cannot provide the
process depth of the originating case, and public-source silence cannot establish that a capability is
absent. These are therefore reconstructions, not six additional case studies and not causal tests of MAGE.

Taken together, those sources support **analytic generalization**: the claim that the MAGE constructs and
mechanisms help explain materially different agentic engineering systems. They do not yet provide
**population-level tests of the predictions above**. Environment moderation, representation leverage,
engineering-capital returns, organizational propagation, and the interaction between model capability and
environment quality remain hypotheses for external study.

That distinction is a strength of the theory, not an embarrassment to hide. The originating case says where
the theory came from. The reconstructions say that the problem travels. The hypotheses say what evidence
would have to come next. [ref:mage-evidence] sets each source against the inference it licenses and the one
it cannot.

## Closing

MAGE does not predict that more governance produces better software. It predicts that abundant
implementation makes the **fit of the engineering environment** increasingly consequential. Useful
representations and known obligations can be engineered before work begins; other needs become visible only
through use. Judgment determines what a failure means, authority determines whether the shared environment
can change in response, and the resulting engineering structures alter what later work has to reconstruct
or decide.

Those structures can create capital, and they can depreciate. A strong environment therefore does more than
add controls: it represents, observes, governs, reconciles, and retires. The predictions above state what
should follow if that account is right. The comparative chapters ask whether independent practice gives the
account anything to explain; [the profession chapter](6.6-education-research-open-problems.html) returns to the harder question of how the field could test it.
