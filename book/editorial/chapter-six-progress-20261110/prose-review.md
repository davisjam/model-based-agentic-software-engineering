# Chapter 6: from faithful production to cumulative progress

Source revision: 511ce1167dbe0f3f4e3574e67906ee6e20025747. Revised 2026-11-10.

The chapter explains why faithful realization, lifecycle economy, and inherited engineering structure require distinct analyses. The reader already knows Modeling, Alignment, governance conversion and the factory/GEE distinction. The new contribution is a testable account of their interactions, ending in an open investment problem.

## Topic-sentence outline

- Three questions move the analysis from individual production to engineering over time.
- Recall the entitlement failure: the client's scalar omitted a distinction available elsewhere in the system.
- Separate encoding intent from faithfully realizing the encoded obligations.
- Lifecycle accounting permits comparison; engineering still has to choose an arrangement.
- The least costly next change can differ from the best investment for the next hundred.
- Prior work changes the GEE inherited by later work.
- An independently characterized dependency model and admission check provide a concrete experimental contrast.
- Engineering routing is a sequential policy choice across reasoning, representation, implementation and assurance.
- Define a fixed body of work and horizon, lifecycle objective, delivery and attention constraints, heterogeneous assurance obligations and GEE inheritance.
- The policy and cost/transition functions are unknown and the available reasoning technology changes.
- Experimental evidence could inform investment; the resulting division of work remains contingent.
