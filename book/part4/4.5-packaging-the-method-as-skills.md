# Packaging the Method as Skills

The method so far has been described as engineering practice: model the relevant knowledge, place authority around stable obligations, evaluate work with independent evidence, and convert recurring judgment into durable structure. Some of that recurring structure can itself be packaged for agents.

<!-- point: a-skill-is-one-packaging-form-for-recurring-procedure | A skill is one packaging form; a recurring procedure becomes engineering capital. | terms: mastery-skill, governance-catalogue -->
A **skill** is one contemporary packaging form. It can carry knowledge, examples, heuristics, decision procedures, triggers, and tools so that a fresh agent does not begin every session cold. That makes skills a form of engineering capital: the organization pays once to articulate a recurring procedure and later work inherits it.

<!-- point: packaging-is-not-authority | Packaging is not authority: prose is guidance, the surrounding machinery may bind. | terms: skill-soft-control, hook-hard-control -->
Do not confuse packaging with authority. A skill made only of prose is reusable guidance. If it invokes a deterministic checker, restricts an action, or participates in a gate, the surrounding machinery may also carry authority. The distinction from Part III still applies after the procedure is packaged.

<!-- point: three-skills-show-one-portable-architecture-not-required-names | Three shipped skills show one portable architecture, not three required MAGE parts. | terms: self-governance, self-operate, self-communicate -->
The companion repository demonstrates the architecture through three mastery skills: self-governance, self-operate, and self-communicate. Together they show one portable way to preserve recurring engineering judgment.

<!-- point: tool-skill-teaches-an-interface-mastery-skill-packages-a-procedure | Tool skill teaches an interface; mastery skill packages a decision procedure. | terms: mastery-skill -->
> **Tool skill.** Teaches an agent how to operate one interface.
>
> **Mastery skill.** Packages domain knowledge plus a repeatable decision procedure for doing a class of
> work well, independent of one particular tool.

A mastery-skill specializes commodity intelligence with institutional engineering judgment: the
general-purpose reasoner supplies the horsepower, and the skill supplies the domain judgment an
experienced engineer brings to this class of work.

Teams are converging on the same reading.[^atlassian] Twilio's example is sharp: an unguided agent asked to "verify users" hand-rolls a six-digit one-time password, while its skill redirects to a dedicated verification product and inherits rate limiting, fraud controls, and routing the operator never thought to request [cite:twilio2026skills]. The encoded procedure is the capital; the class of work becomes inheritable.

[^atlassian]: Atlassian's Jira team reports the same move: it delegates keep-the-lights-on work — flaky tests, feature-flag cleanup, vulnerabilities, accessibility — by turning each into an explicit work item that carries context plus workflow instructions, so a maintenance class becomes delegable once the tacit repair procedure is encoded, not because the agent got clever [cite:atlassian2026maintenance].

## The skill skeleton

<!-- section-terms: per-model-template, orthogonal-models -->

<!-- point: the-three-examples-share-one-construction-pattern | The three examples share one skeleton: base model, orthogonal models, a top-level procedure. | terms: per-model-template, orthogonal-models, model-zoo -->
The three examples share a construction pattern, drawn in [ref:skill-recipe]. A **base model** represents the domain the skill operates over. Additional, largely orthogonal representations capture independent dimensions of the work. A top-level procedure ties those models together: when to invoke them, what questions to ask, which tools to use, and what output must be returned.

<!-- point: the-skeleton-matters-more-than-the-three-names | The skeleton matters more than the three shipped names. | terms: mastery-skill -->
The portable idea is the skeleton: tacit practice becomes a bounded reasoning environment that a new agent can enter repeatedly.

## Deliver the right context—and measure it

<!-- section-terms: standing-context, context-engineering -->

<!-- point: a-reusable-procedure-helps-only-if-the-actor-reaches-it-when-it-matters | A reusable procedure helps only if the actor reaches it when it matters; packaging knowledge is not the same as delivering it. | terms: mastery-skill, standing-context -->
A reusable procedure helps only if the actor reaches it when it matters. Packaging knowledge is not
enough; it has to reach the reasoner when relevant, without drowning the reasoner in everything else.

<!-- point: loading-everything-just-moves-the-navigation-problem | The naive answer — load everything into standing context — merely moves the navigation problem inside the reasoner. | terms: standing-context -->
The naive answer is to load everything: put every rule, architecture note, failure lesson, tool
instruction, and procedure into the agent's standing context and trust attention to sort it out. That
merely moves the navigation problem. Use two layers instead, drawn in [ref:context-two-layers].

<!-- label: context-two-layers -->
<!-- figure: assets/deliver-context-two-layer.svg | *Two layers of context.* Engineering knowledge — models, rules, procedures — splits into a small standing context (mission, compact method, the few policies whose salience must survive every task) and a task-relevant slice retrieved when the work calls for it. Both feed the agent's context; a lifecycle event can re-surface what matters now. [short: Two layers of context — standing context (always resident) and a task-relevant slice retrieved on demand feed the agent] -->

<!-- point: keep-standing-context-small-enough-to-genuinely-remain-standing | Keep standing context small enough that it can genuinely remain standing; retrieve richer slices according to the work. | terms: standing-context, context-engineering -->
<!-- index-def: standing-context -->
Standing context should contain the mission, compact method, and the few policies whose salience must
survive every task. Retrieve richer model slices, procedures, examples, and local constraints
according to the work. Where a relevant event can
be observed mechanically, push the needed procedure into view rather than hoping the actor remembers
to pull it.

<!-- point: context-has-a-cost-more-guidance-is-not-automatically-better | Context has a cost: it consumes tokens, attention, and reasoning capacity, and stale guidance can actively misdirect work. | terms: context-engineering, teetering-tower -->
But context has a cost. More guidance is not automatically better guidance. It consumes tokens,
attention, and reasoning capacity, and stale guidance can actively misdirect work.

<!-- point: treat-context-delivery-as-an-engineering-intervention-and-measure-its-effect | Treat context delivery as an engineering intervention and measure its effect: compare with and without, task success first, then diagnostics. | terms: measure-one-level-deeper, context-engineering -->
So treat context delivery as an engineering intervention and measure its effect. Compare performance
with and without the added context. Measure task success first; then use diagnostic measures —
tokens, navigation steps, tool calls, files opened, retries, or time — where they illuminate *why*
performance changed. A context mechanism that consumes more reasoning budget without improving the
engineering outcome has not earned its place.

<!-- point: the-model-based-pilot-is-one-example-of-testing-whether-engineered-context-does-what-we-claim | The model-based engineering pilot is one example of testing whether engineered context does what we claim, not the point itself. | terms: context-engineering -->
The model-based software-engineering pilot is one example: run the same task against different
model-and-context conditions and reasoner classes, and ask whether the supplied representation
actually changes navigation cost or task success. What is portable is the discipline of testing
whether engineered context does what we claim it does.
[Part V](5.1-the-problem-and-the-bar.html) carries that pilot with its data and its limitations.

<!-- point: context-delivery-changes-what-the-reasoner-can-see-not-what-the-environment-permits | Context delivery changes what the reasoner can see; it does not, by itself, change what the environment will permit — the bridge back to Part III. | terms: skill-soft-control, hook-hard-control -->
**Context delivery changes what the reasoner can see; by itself, it does not change what the
environment permits.** A richer slice of the right rules makes a good action easier to find; only a
constraint, validator, or gate makes a bad action unavailable.

## Self-Governance: Engineering the Environment

<!-- section-terms: self-governance, governance-catalogue -->

<!-- point: recurrence-is-evidence-the-environment-may-be-missing-something-durable | Recurrence is evidence the environment may be missing something durable — not always a new control. | terms: self-governance, governance-conversion -->
<!-- index-def: self-governance -->
Self-governance packages Modeling and Alignment as a procedure the agent can apply to its own engineering work. Recognize the situation, decide what to model and what to make authoritative, choose a move, act through the environment, weigh the evidence, and keep what should be inherited. Converting a recurring failure is one branch of that loop, not its definition. A recurrence is evidence the environment may be missing something durable — but the missing thing is not always a new control. It may be a model, an explicit obligation, better evidence, a validator, a constraint, a gate, or simply a decision procedure worth preserving.

<!-- point: the-skill-carries-a-catalogue-and-a-procedure-for-fitting-it | The skill carries a catalogue of mechanisms and a procedure for deciding which one fits. | terms: governance-catalogue -->
<!-- index-def: governance-catalogue -->
The skill therefore carries two assets: a catalogue of mechanisms learned from prior failures and a procedure for deciding whether any of them fits the present one. Its value comes from making accumulated engineering experience available to a fresh agent. Instead of relying on the agent's broad software-engineering priors, the skill supplies the MAGE-specific judgment needed to decide what to do here.

<!-- point: rules-bind-only-where-a-mechanism-gives-them-consequence | Rules in boot context bind only where a mechanism gives them consequence. | terms: governed-environment, ex-ante-governance -->
The project's boot context can preserve mission, method, and concise rules, but treat those rules accurately. Mission orients. Method teaches judgment. Rules state project policy. **They bind only where an environmental mechanism gives them consequence.** A numbered sentence in an always-loaded file is still guidance unless something outside the producing agent enforces it.

Underneath the catalogue sit a few reflexes the skill applies on every touch. Three of them:

> ### Self-governance — three principles
>
> - **Convert, don't merely repair.** Ask what recurring expenditure of judgment future work should
>   inherit.
> - **Guidance aims; authority binds.** Never describe prose as enforced merely because every agent sees
>   it.
> - **Prefer prevention when the legitimate action space can be narrowed structurally.**

<!-- point: a-passive-skill-needs-a-hook-and-surfaces-the-design-forks | A passive skill needs a hook to fire it; it resolves the classifiable cases and surfaces the design forks. | terms: skill-soft-control, hook-hard-control, reflection-hook -->
A skill on its own is passive: it sits and waits to be invoked, and an agent heads-down in the work will not stop to invoke it. A hook brings it to life — the reflection fired often enough that a repeat surfaces while its cause is still fresh. The hook fires, the skill reaches into its catalogue, resolves the mechanically classifiable cases, and **surfaces the design forks** — "we keep hitting this; here are two ideas, which should I try?" — then waits. The hook makes the reflection happen; the catalogue supplies the options; the judgment call stays yours. The skill therefore surfaces the design fork rather than silently installing a mechanism; the engineer retains the judgment about what matters enough to preserve.

<!-- point: avoid-a-teetering-tower-capital-can-depreciate | Governance is capital that can depreciate: watch for the teetering tower. | terms: teetering-tower, right-level-of-enforcement -->
<!-- index-def: teetering-tower -->
Governance is capital, and capital can depreciate. Over-investment turns governance maintenance into its own recurring cost. Three symptoms say the return has gone negative: upkeep crowds out product work; the checks cry wolf, flagging more than they catch; or the guarded failure is both cheap and rare. Each is a control whose upkeep outweighs its benefit. Size it the way [Migrating to MAGE](4.2-brownfield.html) sizes any control — against the cost and the frequency of the failure it prevents — and retire the assets that no longer earn their cost.

Even a soft mechanism can be admitted like a hard one. NVIDIA treats a skill as a deployable capability, not a static prompt, and runs it through an eight-stage supply chain — source, review, scan, evaluate, skill card, sign, catalog, sync — where scanning looks for agent-specific threats (hidden instructions, trigger abuse, excessive agency, tool poisoning, purpose/access mismatch), not only conventional software risk [cite:nvidia2026verifiedskills]. The prose is knowledge capital; the signing and gating around it are where authority is added.

## Self-Operate: Running the Environment

<!-- section-terms: self-operate, lifecycle -->

<!-- point: self-operate-packages-the-previous-chapters-operating-discipline | Self-operate packages the previous chapter's lifecycles, recovery paths, and runbook step types. | terms: self-operate, lifecycle, runbook -->
<!-- index-def: self-operate -->
Self-operate packages the previous chapter's lifecycles, recovery paths, runbook step types, and operating heuristics. Its base models describe recurring repository activities; its procedure tells an agent how to orient against the healthy path, run deterministic steps through tools, dispatch delegable judgment with a prepared brief, and surface irreducible judgment to the operator.

<!-- point: the-skill-makes-operating-procedure-repeatable-not-authoritative | The skill makes operating procedure repeatable; it does not replace the underlying tools or gates. | terms: self-operate, done-is-a-claim -->
The skill is useful because operating procedure otherwise has to be reconstructed at every fresh session. It does not replace the underlying tools, state machines, validators, or gates. It makes their use repeatable.

Its boundary is the other half of the same interface. Self-operate routes an operating condition to a typed runbook and runs it; when a condition instead exposes a deficiency in the models, mechanisms, skills, or lifecycle itself, self-operate returns the *engineering problem* — with evidence, not a redesign — up to self-governance. It runs the environment; it does not re-engineer it.

The two mastery skills divide the work by the question each answers. [ref:sg-so-split] sets the split side by side.

<!-- label: sg-so-split -->
<!-- table: *The two mastery skills, by facet.* Self-governance asks how the environment should be engineered and produces durable capital; self-operate asks how to run a lifecycle and produces a completed operation or returned evidence. Each hands the other exactly one thing: design flows down to be run, and engineering deficiencies flow back up to be fixed. [short: Governance versus operate] -->
| Facet | **Self-governance** | **Self-operate** |
|---|---|---|
| The question it answers | *How should this be engineered?* | *How do I operate this lifecycle?* |
| Acts on | the environment — models, mechanisms, skills | the running lifecycles — dispatch, land, deploy, recover |
| Produces | durable engineering capital | a completed operation, or returned evidence |
| Hands off | *design* down to self-operate to run | *engineering deficiencies* up to self-governance to fix |

The interface is two-way and explicit: self-governance delegates execution downward; self-operate returns engineering problems upward. Neither absorbs the other — a mechanism lives in the governance census and is *run* by operations, the same thing seen design-time versus run-time.

## Self-communicate: a boundary test

<!-- section-terms: self-communicate, lexicon -->

<!-- point: self-communicate-packages-a-farther-craft-through-the-same-skeleton | Self-communicate packages a farther craft — prose and drawing — through the same skeleton. | terms: self-communicate, documentation-hierarchy -->
<!-- index-def: self-communicate -->
Self-communicate packages a different craft: engineer-facing prose and technical drawing. Its value here is mostly architectural. Governance and operations sit close to the method's core; technical communication is farther away, yet the same skeleton still works — a domain model, orthogonal representations of the craft, and a decision procedure that tells the agent how to apply them.

<!-- point: the-third-skill-is-a-boundary-test-not-a-third-pillar | The third skill is a boundary test for the architecture, not a third pillar of MAGE. | terms: self-communicate, mastery-skill -->
That makes self-communicate a useful boundary test for the skill architecture, not a third pillar of MAGE. It shows that the same packaging pattern can extend beyond governance and operations; the appendix and companion repository carry the procedure itself.

Reusable agent procedures are one concrete form of engineering capital, and the industry is finding the pattern in parallel. Part V's [MAGE in the Wild](5.5-mage-in-the-wild.html) sets six such systems side by side.

<!-- worked-examples: engineering-capital -->
### Example — Shopify {other}
Shopify mines judgment from prior sessions into defaults later work inherits. Every agent session executes in public and becomes a searchable artifact, and a shared session is mined into a skill and a default that every later agent starts from. The copyable move is small: make the work visible, then mine the repeated judgment into a skill, so the environment gets incrementally smarter with each pass instead of every session starting cold.

### Example — Zenseact {other}
Zenseact shows the organizational variant, where the same capital is owned by many teams at once. A central team owns the runtime and the safety envelope, while each domain team contributes an agent as little more than a directory holding a config and a skill. Domain expertise accretes as reusable skills a router surfaces on demand, so the organization centralizes the mechanism and decentralizes the judgment.

<!-- takeaway -->
Reusable agent procedures are one concrete form of engineering capital. Shopify shows judgment mined from prior sessions into defaults later work inherits; Zenseact shows the organizational variant, where domain teams contribute reusable expertise to a centrally governed runtime. The portable move is to preserve recurring procedure without pretending that packaging alone grants authority.
