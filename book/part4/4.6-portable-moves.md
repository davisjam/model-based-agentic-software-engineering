# Closing: The Portable Moves

<!-- thesisbox -->
> ### THE PORTABLE MOVES
>
> Strip away the one system that produced the method and a small set of transferable moves is left.
> **You can run every move below on a codebase this book has never seen.**

The chapters before this one taught the method through one deep case and a handful of outside teams. This closing strips the case away. What remains is the part that ports: a few moves you can carry to your own repository without adopting anyone's platform, tooling, or story.

<!-- point: the-method-reduces-to-a-few-moves-over-two-theses | The method reduces to a few moves over Model and Align, with Convert closing the loop. | terms: thesis-modeling, thesis-alignment, governance-conversion -->
Everything here descends from one shape. Modeling makes intent explicit, Alignment gives that intent authority, and the failures the work reveals feed back into both — so each pass begins from a stronger position than the last. Five moves carry that shape. Each keeps its one-line statement and names the chapter that teaches it in full.

<!-- point: model-the-smallest-thing-that-makes-the-question-answerable | Model the smallest thing that makes the question answerable. | terms: thesis-modeling, model-as-map -->
**Model the smallest thing that makes the question answerable.** A model earns its keep by making one engineering question checkable, not by picturing the whole system. Find the question, model exactly enough to answer it, and stop. ([The MAGE Workflow](4.1-the-mage-workflow.html).)

<!-- point: give-important-properties-independent-authority | Give important properties independent authority. | terms: thesis-alignment, constraint -->
**Give important properties independent authority.** A property you care about should be held by the environment, not by whoever remembers to check it. State it as an invariant and let a mechanism — a type, a gate, an oracle — hold the line whether or not the agent cooperates. ([Validating Change](4.3-validating-change.html), [The MAGE Workflow](4.1-the-mage-workflow.html).)

<!-- point: audit-drain-promote-when-introducing-a-new-invariant | Audit, drain, promote when introducing a new invariant. | terms: make-lint-blocking, thesis-alignment -->
**Audit → drain → promote when introducing a new invariant.** A valid future rule cannot always be imposed as a valid present one. Let the rule enter audit-only, drain the existing violations under tests, and only then turn it blocking. ([Migrating to MAGE](4.2-brownfield.html).)

<!-- point: validate-the-class-not-merely-the-example | Validate the class, not merely the example. | terms: generative-validation, thesis-alignment -->
**Validate the class, not merely the example.** An example pins one point; a property states the law over the space and blunts the agent's move to special-case the test. Generate evidence built to falsify the claim, and admit only what survives. ([Validating Change](4.3-validating-change.html).)

<!-- point: convert-recurring-judgment-into-infrastructure | Convert recurring judgment into infrastructure. | terms: governance-conversion, mastery-skill -->
**Convert recurring judgment into infrastructure.** The second time you make the same call by hand, it has earned a mechanism. Turn the recurring judgment into a lint, a gate, a runbook, or a skill — spend it once and collect it free on every later change. ([The MAGE Workflow](4.1-the-mage-workflow.html#the-conversion-loop), [The Skills](4.4-the-skills.html).)

The method ran end to end across this Part, and every chapter of it was a conversion: brownfield adoption is conversion at the scale of a whole legacy tree, the skills are conversions packaged to travel, the generative checks are conversions worn as tests, and the loop underneath turns each new failure into the next mechanism.
