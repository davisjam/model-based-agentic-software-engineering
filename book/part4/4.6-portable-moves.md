# Closing: The Portable Moves

<!-- thesisbox -->
> ### THE PORTABLE MOVES
>
> Strip away DocAble, the companion repository, and the current generation of agent harnesses, and a
> small set of moves remains. These are the parts of the method meant to port.

Strip away DocAble, the companion repository, and the current generation of agent harnesses, and a small set of moves remains. These are the parts of the method meant to port.

<!-- point: model-the-smallest-thing-that-makes-the-question-answerable | Model at the coarsest grain that makes the engineering question answerable. | terms: thesis-modeling, model-as-map -->
**Model at the coarsest grain that makes the engineering question answerable.** Do not model the whole system because a model sounds sophisticated. Identify the recurring question, externalize enough structure to answer it without reconstructing the implementation, and stop when additional detail no longer pays.

<!-- point: give-important-properties-independent-authority | Place authority at the earliest boundary where the obligation is legible and enforceable. | terms: thesis-alignment, constraint -->
**Place authority at the earliest boundary where the obligation is legible and enforceable.** Guidance can aim an agent before that point; local controls can sometimes act without a rich system model. Give binding consequence only where the environment has the semantics and evidence needed to decide the obligation honestly.

<!-- point: audit-drain-promote-when-introducing-a-new-invariant | Audit, drain, promote when introducing a future invariant into inherited reality. | terms: make-lint-blocking, thesis-alignment -->
**Audit → Drain → Promote when introducing a future invariant into inherited reality.** A rule worth enforcing tomorrow may be violated everywhere today. Observe first, repair the inherited findings under evidence, then grant the mechanism authority once the estate can satisfy the rule without breaking work in flight.

<!-- point: validate-the-class-not-merely-the-example | Generate evidence capable of falsifying the claim. | terms: generative-validation, thesis-alignment -->
**Generate evidence capable of falsifying the claim.** Do not rely on the producing agent's report that its work is correct. Choose an oracle appropriate to the obligation, search beyond the examples already known, and ask whether the search actually reached the semantic region the claim covers.

<!-- point: convert-recurring-judgment-into-infrastructure | Convert recurring judgment into engineering capital when the return justifies the upkeep. | terms: governance-conversion, mastery-skill -->
**Convert recurring judgment into engineering capital when the return justifies the upkeep.** A repeated reconstruction may earn a model; a repeated review may earn a validator; a repeated unsafe action may earn a constraint; a repeated procedure may earn a skill or runbook. Spend judgment once when future work can profit from inheriting it — but remember that capital depreciates. Maintain useful assets and retire the ones that no longer earn their cost.

<!-- point: the-moves-can-themselves-be-represented | The moves here are written for the engineer; a mastery-skill can represent the situations, alternatives, and selection criteria themselves. | terms: mastery-skill, self-governance -->
These moves are written for the engineer to apply. They can also be *represented*: a mastery-skill can externalize the situations that trigger each move, the alternatives available in each, and the criteria that select among them — the method turned into something an agent reads and reasons through rather than a habit a person carries. [appendix: appendix-skill-recipe] shows this concretely in self-governance, which builds exactly this representation of the method from the skill recipe.
