# How to read this book

The parts compose in one conceptual progression. **Part 1** establishes the changed economics — why
code got cheap, and the two theses everything rests on. **Parts 2 and 3** develop the two theses in
causal order: first representation, then authority — you can only make binding what you have first
made explicit, so the models come before the controls that ride them. **Part 4** joins them into the
MAGE method and tests its portability by reconstructing several independently evolved engineering
systems through the same lens. **Part 5** then returns to the system from which MAGE was induced,
DocAble, and opens it end to end. **Part 6** asks what survives those encounters: the refined theory,
its scope, its relationship to established software and systems engineering, and what commodity
intelligence changes about the profession. [ref:book-map] shows the whole path and the routes through it.

A reference back matter — appendices, about-the-author, colophon — sits beside the argument, not
after it. The appendices are a working reference, not the argument's next stage: consult a pattern
when you meet its failure, the way you would any design-pattern reference.

Read it straight through and the argument builds in order. There are also three faster ways in:

- **Do it Monday:** Part 1 → Part 4 → the appendices.
- **Convince me first:** Part 1 → Part 5 → Part 6.
- **I want the theory:** Part 1 → Part 2 → Part 3 → Part 6.

Read the book once, front to back, and you learn the method; return to it while you build, and it
works a second way. The first pass is sequential — each part rests on the one before it, from the two
theses through the method and into the DocAble case, then into the closing theory. Every pass after
that is not: once you are standing up a governed environment of your own, the book turns into a
reference you open at the page you need, and the appendices carry that second mode. Their opener
explains how the references fit together.
