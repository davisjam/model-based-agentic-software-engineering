# What This Book Argues

This book makes six central claims, and they are not a loose list. They are one causal chain — the
same descent this book opens with, read top to bottom — each claim the reason the next one follows.
The chapters ahead defend them; this page only names them, so you can watch each being tested as you
read.

> Commodity intelligence → a reasoning problem → Modeling → Alignment → Governance conversion →
> engineering capital → a changed profession.

1. **Commodity intelligence reorganizes engineering.** When implementation becomes abundant, engineering reorganizes around judgment, representations, evidence, authority, and governance. The engineer's leverage is no longer in the typing.
2. **Software engineering is fundamentally a reasoning problem.** Large systems exceed the reasoning horizon of humans and agents alike. Models exist to extend reasoning, not merely to document software. That, not raw implementation speed, is what limits how large a system a fleet of agents can build.
3. **The Modeling Thesis.** Explicit models externalize engineering intent into representations that humans and agents can reason through, and they create the surfaces on which engineering policy can later act. A model is not documentation pinned beside the code; it is the thing you reason through.
4. **The Alignment Thesis.** Engineering quality should emerge from authoritative mechanisms embedded in the environment rather than repeated human inspection — the constraints, sensors, validators, and gates that hold every change to the standard automatically.
5. **Governance Conversion.** Every recurring failure should become durable engineering infrastructure, producing engineering capital instead of engineering churn. The lesson is captured once, in something that makes the mistake impossible or catches it every time, so it is never relearned; that capital lowers the future cost of change, so effort compounds for as long as the capital stays fit.
6. **The profession changes.** Engineers increasingly author models, encode policy, and grow governed engineering environments rather than primarily writing implementation.

The rest of the book exists to earn these.
