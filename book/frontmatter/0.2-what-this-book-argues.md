# What This Book Argues

This book makes six central claims. Together they describe the engineering problem created by scale, what commodity intelligence changes, and the method MAGE proposes in response.

1. **Commodity intelligence changes the economics of software engineering.** Implementation capacity is becoming abundant relative to engineering judgment. As that constraint loosens, engineering effort shifts toward what remains scarce: deciding what should be built, representing the system well enough to reason about it, producing evidence, and giving consequential intent authority.
2. **Scale creates a reasoning problem.** Large software systems already exceed the reasoning horizon of humans; agents inherit the same problem. Software engineering has always answered scale with abstraction. Commodity intelligence does not remove that need. It makes the quality of the representations through which work proceeds increasingly consequential.
3. **The Modeling Principle.** Externalize engineering knowledge and intent into explicit, purposeful models that preserve what an engineering question needs and suppress what it does not. Models let humans and agents reason about larger properties without repeatedly reconstructing them from implementation, and they make more consequential facts available to tools.
4. **The Alignment Principle.** Give important engineering obligations authority over autonomous work. Constraints, sensors, validators, and gates provide the mechanisms. Alignment does not require a comprehensive model of the system: a sandbox, a test, or a permission boundary can already hold a local rule. Explicit models expand what can be stated and checked; where the necessary abstraction is absent, human judgment often fills the gap.
5. **Governance conversion turns recurring judgment into durable engineering structure.** When a failure exposes missing knowledge or an unenforced obligation, encode the lesson into a model, procedure, or mechanism that future work can inherit. Useful durable structure becomes engineering capital when it continues to lower the future cost or uncertainty of change.
6. **The profession changes.** Engineers increasingly design the representations, evidence, policies, and governed environments through which abundant implementation capacity can be used safely. Writing implementation remains engineering work; it is no longer where all of the leverage resides.
