# The MAGE Method at a Glance

MAGE is one method with two theses and a conversion that grows an environment around them. To help you understand the full argument, [ref:mage-method] shows the whole of it on a single page and how the parts relate to each other — the vocabulary this book builds, laid out in the order the method runs.

<!-- label: mage-method -->
<!-- figure: assets/mage-method.svg | The whole method as one fork. Commodity intelligence — broad, cheap, probabilistic — meets a reasoning-horizon problem: the system won't fit the window. Two gates decide what follows. Pass the *Modeling Thesis*, make knowledge and intent explicit, and one step leads to structured, executable models. A short, heavy edge, *creates authority surfaces*, carries those models into the *Alignment Thesis*, where represented intent becomes authority through constraints, sensors, validators, and gates, and effort compounds into engineering capital. Fail either gate — intent left implicit, or models left inert — and the work dissipates into engineering churn: one box, two ways to reach it. Governance conversion closes the loop, turning failure into structure that enriches either side, and the descent repeats. -->

The Alignment Thesis fills the environment with governance mechanisms of four archetypes, each named for the role it plays rather than the outcome it serves. [ref:mechanism-archetypes] names the four and the role that distinguishes each.

<!-- label: mechanism-archetypes -->
| Mechanism | Role |
|---|---|
| **Constraint** | restricts actions |
| **Sensor** | observes violations |
| **Validator** | evaluates evidence |
| **Gate** | controls admission |

The method above runs on a theory the closing chapters develop in full. That theory also has a dynamics view — a causal model of how governed velocity compounds into progress or dissipates into churn ([ref:mage-dynamic-model]) — earned once the theses and what compounds have been established; it lands in the opening chapter of the closing part.
