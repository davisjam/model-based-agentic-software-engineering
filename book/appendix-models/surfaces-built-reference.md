The **surfaces built** reference page — the present-tense mirror of the Part II → Part III
handoff. Part II handed Alignment seven typed seams (six models and the scenario that joins them),
each naming, in role and shape, an authority surface for this Part to build. This page is the
receipt: for every model, the property Part II owned and the constraint · sensor · validator · gate
Part III built over it, with the chapter that worked it. The chapter *Growing the Governed
Environment* (3.4) states the headline — **five governed, two gaps** — and points here for the full
row-by-row account.

**Five rows close into a gate that holds. Two stay OPEN** — modeled but not yet governed, in the
exact bounded sense 3.4 draws: sensed and logged, not yet gated. The table marks the two so, and
points them back to that gap rather than papering a fabricated gate over either. Read this receipt
([ref:surfaces-built]) against the Model-and-Property pairing that
[Part II handed forward](2.8-joining-models-around-a-scenario.html) — and the story runs continuous across the Part boundary:
the model and the property unchanged, the authority now built rather than promised.

The two OPEN rows are not holes the book patches with a gate it does not have. They are the honest
edge of the method, named in full in 3.4's *observed-only* leaf: the cost ceiling closes by
conversion once an overrun recurs often enough to earn the gate; the reachability proof waits on the
exhaustive state-walk a later Part builds. Naming both as *modeled but not yet governed* keeps the
ledger honest — it admits what the method specified and has not yet enforced.
