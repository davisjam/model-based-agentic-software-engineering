# Coda: Engineering the Factory

<!-- point: process-design-and-tolerance-describe-the-differences-without-a-maturity-model | The cases show software production reorganizing around capable agents, and the distinction between process design and tolerance describes how they differ without treating those differences as stages of a common maturity model. | terms: tolerance -->
The cases in this chapter show several ways that software production is reorganizing around capable
agents. Contemporary factories differ in where they place engineering knowledge, how they arrange
production, how they divide work between people and agents, and how they evaluate what production
yields. The distinction between process design and tolerance gives us a vocabulary for describing
those differences without treating them as stages of a common maturity model — factory designers can
reasonably place engineering control in different parts of the production system.

<!-- point: process-design-and-tolerance-give-machinery-for-the-theory-chapter | The same distinction supplies machinery for the theory chapter: how engineering work is redistributed as realization becomes cheaper, why some judgments move into durable production structures, and where representation, evidence, and attention cost. | terms: tolerance -->
It also gives us machinery for going further. Process design asks how work, information, authority,
evidence, and admission are arranged around a capable fabricator. Tolerance asks which variation the
factory permits and which properties it must keep under engineering control. Together, those ideas
let us model how engineering work is redistributed as realization becomes cheaper and more capable,
why some judgments are worth moving into durable production structures, and where the costs of
representation, evidence, and human attention should appear. {{chapter:theory}} develops a
mathematical model of agentic engineering and a dynamic model of engineering practice, then uses
them to derive predictions about how agent capability, engineering structure, and the economics of
controlled change interact.
