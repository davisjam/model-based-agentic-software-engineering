The eight blueprints in this appendix address recurring engineering problems in DocAble: connecting service declarations to deployment permissions; controlling document mutations; substituting reasoning engines; governing coding-agent assignments; selecting regression tests; maintaining computation dependencies; protecting job ownership and recovery; and designing execution within resource bounds. Each explains a construction that makes selected engineering knowledge usable by agents, tools, and checks.

Each blueprint follows a common format, drawing on the design-pattern tradition exemplified by Gamma, Helm, Johnson, and Vlissides [cite: gof1994]. *Problem* identifies the failure or capability that motivates the arrangement. *Conceptual Design* explains how its representations and mechanisms interact. *Engineering Models* identifies the representations, their meanings and authority, the questions they answer, and their consumers. *Realization* describes how to construct, operate, and verify the arrangement, including the evidence needed to establish that its mechanisms work. *Adaptation and Limits* explains where the arrangement applies and what it does not establish. [ref:chapter:appendix-a-designing-your-own-blueprint] applies this format to a new engineering problem.

The designs are grounded in DocAble, with construction advice for analogous arrangements in other systems. The engineer chooses the obligations, establishes what each mechanism can decide, and retains judgments for which the available evidence is insufficient. Appendix B complements these blueprints with practical guidance for choosing where to begin, migrating an existing environment, operating it, and measuring whether it pays.
