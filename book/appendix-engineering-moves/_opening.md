**Worked examples in problem and solution.** The chapters teach the MAGE method. This appendix asks a more practical question: what does the same engineering judgment look like when the implementation changes?

The pages that follow collect recurring engineering problems and the moves that address them. Each move appears through two different realizations. The examples are not recipes. They exist to expose the structure shared by otherwise unlike mechanisms, so you can recognize the same problem in your own system and design a fitting response.

Read each page in one direction:

**Problem → Move → two realizations.**

The distinction carries the appendix. A **move** is transferable engineering judgment — close an action surface, derive obligations from a model, deliver policy where a decision happens. A **mechanism** is one concrete realization of that judgment: a typed mutation API, a coverage census, a context-injection hook. The mechanism may be peculiar to its system. The move travels.

These pages stay selective. They do not reproduce the full mechanism repertoire, and they do not repeat the theory developed in Parts II–IV. Appendix A shows how mechanisms compose into engineering capabilities. This appendix shows how one move takes different forms. The online MAGE Mechanism Catalog carries the complete implementation catalogue.

The test of understanding is not whether you can reproduce DocAble. It is whether, when your own environment starts paying repeatedly for the same engineering problem, you can recognize its shape and invent the mechanism your system needs.
