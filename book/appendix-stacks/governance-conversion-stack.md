## The capability

**Turn recurring failure into durable engineering capital — and keep the control estate itself
understandable.** A governed environment accumulates controls; this stack keeps that growing estate legible
and feeds each recurring failure back into it as a new or stronger control.

## When this stack earns its keep

Reach for it when:

- **The controls have grown their own complexity** — they overlap, depend on one another, go stale, and
  carry blast radius nobody has mapped.
- **The same failure keeps recurring**, and each fix is a one-off patch rather than a durable control.
- **Lessons live in institutional memory** instead of in the environment, so they leave when the person who
  learned them does.

Make the control estate explicit, query it for gaps and coupling, convert a recurring failure into a durable
control, and fold that control back into the estate so the model changes with it.

## Constituent moves

| Move | Role |
|---|---|
| **MODEL** | Make the control estate explicit — rules, gates, models, sensors. |
| **EXPOSE** | Query the estate for gaps, staleness, and coupling. |
| **CONVERT** | Interpret a recurring failure class into a new or strengthened control. |
| **UPDATE** | Fold the control back into the estate; the model changes with it. |

## Why these travel together

A growing governed environment develops its own complexity. Controls overlap. They depend on one another,
they go stale, they acquire blast radius. The estate becomes the thing that is now hard to reason about.

The answer is the move applied everywhere else in this material: model the thing that has become hard to
reason about. Once the control estate is explicit, a recurring failure can be interpreted against it — a
failure worth converting becomes a new or strengthened mechanism, and the model of the estate updates to
match.

This is the stack most directly tied to engineering capital. When the environment itself changes, a lesson
stops depending on whoever remembers it. Rule registries, dependency graphs, indexes, metadata — those are
possible implementations of the four moves, not six mandatory stages the stack must march through.
