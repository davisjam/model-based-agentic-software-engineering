# Summary

{{chapter:modeling}} examined how engineering models express knowledge and intent. This chapter examined how we can establish mechanisms that evaluate and enforce the obligations expressed in those models.

1.  **Distinguish guidance from enforcement.** Guidance influences an agent’s work. Enforcement restricts what can happen or determines whether work may proceed. Correspondence, conformance, and acceptance are different engineering judgments.

2.  **Choose mechanisms suited to the obligation.** Constraints restrict actions or states, sensors produce evidence, and gates enforce decisions. Greater semantic commitment can enable mechanical analysis, generation, and derivation of checks. We must also evaluate the machinery itself.

3.  **Choose appropriate enforcement boundaries.** Establish mechanisms where sufficient evidence is available and where their decisions can still have the required effect. Preserve implementation freedom where its restriction has no engineering justification.

4.  **Preserve engineering knowledge for subsequent work.** Known obligations and lessons from failures can motivate reusable models and controls. These become engineering capital when they reduce the cost or uncertainty of later engineering.

5.  **Model the governance machinery.** Controls can interfere with one another, leave obligations uncovered, or outlive their purpose. The engineering environment has become a system that warrants modeling and maintenance.

> ### Alignment Principle {#alignment-principle}
>
> Make selected engineering obligations enforceable through constraints that restrict actions or states, sensors that produce evidence, and gates that evaluate evidence and control whether work may proceed.
