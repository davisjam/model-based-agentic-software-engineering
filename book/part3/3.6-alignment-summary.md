# Summary

Modeling establishes what an engineered system should satisfy; Alignment determines how selected obligations will be upheld as agents work. Constraints restrict available actions or states, sensors produce evidence, and gates use evidence to decide what may proceed. Their effectiveness depends on choosing obligations independently of the work being judged, placing mechanisms where those obligations can be evaluated, and understanding the limits of the resulting assurance.

1.  **Enforcement makes an obligation more than a request.** Guidance tells an agent how it should act. Enforcement determines which actions the environment permits or which results it accepts.

2.  **Enforce at the earliest decidable boundary.** A constraint can prohibit an action as soon as the relevant facts are available. An obligation about the result may have to wait until a sensor can produce the evidence a gate needs.

3.  **Correspondence is not correctness.** A model and its implementation can agree and share the same error. To judge correctness, engineers need an independent obligation and evidence capable of revealing a violation.

4.  **Convert recurring judgment into durable structure.** When a failure or repeated decision reveals something future work should inherit, preserve it in a model, check, or control. That structure becomes engineering capital when subsequent work benefits from it.

5.  **Govern the governance machinery.** Controls can leave an obligation uncovered, interfere with one another, or become too costly to maintain. Engineers need to know which controls exist, how they interact, and which obligations they govern.

> ### Alignment Principle {#alignment-principle}
>
> Make selected engineering obligations enforceable through constraints that restrict actions or states, sensors that produce evidence, and gates that evaluate evidence and control whether work may proceed.

Alignment makes selected obligations dependable without prescribing every choice involved in satisfying them. Agents can investigate, design, and implement within the freedom those obligations preserve; engineers remain responsible for the resulting system. They decide what must hold and whether the available evidence justifies acceptance. Mechanization is valuable where it reduces repeated judgment or prevents known failures. Beyond those boundaries, reasoning remains necessary.
