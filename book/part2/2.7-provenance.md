# Provenance Models: What Happened?

When an autonomous system changes a user's artifact, completion is not the only fact worth preserving.
Engineers and users may need to know what changed, which operation changed it, and why. A provenance
model represents that history as structured evidence rather than prose reconstructed after the fact.

DocAble represents consequential mutations as structured attribution rather than relying solely on a
narrative assembled after processing.

> #### Attribution model · Provenance
>
> - **Engineering question** — What happened to this artifact, which operation did it, and what evidence
>   records the change?
> - **Model** — a run made of operations, each naming its target, its mutation, the pass that made it,
>   and the evidence it left.
> - **Property** — human-facing explanations are *derived* from one structured action record, not
>   maintained as an independent account.
> - **Quality attribute** — auditability, debuggability, and consistency between what changed and what is
>   reported.

<!-- point: a-provenance-model-selects-events-that-matter-to-explanation-not-a-full-trace | The attribution model records the events that matter to engineering explanation and audit, not a trace of every function call. -->
[ref:attribution-model] draws a reduced provenance model: a run contains operations, and each operation
names a target, a mutation, the pass responsible, and the evidence it left. This is not a trace of every
function call — that would reproduce implementation history rather than model it. The representation
selects the events that matter to engineering explanation and audit.

<!-- point: record-once-derive-the-explanations | A human-facing explanation is derived from the structured action record rather than maintained as a second, independently-written account. -->
From those events the system derives its other views. A human-facing explanation is not maintained as an
independent account of what the system did; it is *derived* from the structured action record.
**Record once; derive the explanations.** [ref:provenance-record-vs-views] draws that direction: one
action record feeding an engineering audit and a customer changelog, rather than each story written and
maintained on its own. That architecture reduces a familiar source of inconsistency — implementation
changing while the documentation describing it follows a separate path.

<!-- point: a-provenance-model-does-not-guarantee-every-operation-records-its-attribution | Whether every consequential operation actually records its attribution is an Alignment question, not something the provenance model itself guarantees. -->
There is an important limit, and it is worth stating precisely. A provenance model does not guarantee
that every consequential operation actually records its attribution. That is an Alignment question.
DocAble addresses it separately, by requiring typed mutation verbs to emit attribution records and
checking that obligation structurally; Part III examines that mechanism. The distinction is worth
holding: **Part II models what evidence should exist; Part III decides whether producing that evidence is
optional.**

The next and final chapter of this Part steps back from any single model to ask how the six fit together
as a system.
