# Measurement Models: How Much, How Fast, How Good?

Quantitative engineering begins by deciding which quantities matter and how they relate. Raw telemetry
is not yet a model. A pile of request logs and token counts becomes a measurement model only when an
engineer names the quantities worth relating and the bounds worth comparing against.

DocAble's use of generative AI makes this concrete. A production job may consume a variable amount of
model capacity and incur variable cost. The system therefore represents several related quantities rather
than treating individual API calls as isolated events.

> #### GenAI cost and capacity · Measurement
>
> - **Engineering question** — How much capacity is this workload consuming, what does a unit of work
>   cost, and how close is demand to an operating or budget envelope?
> - **Model** — a small web of related quantities: generation work resolves into usage and concurrency,
>   which resolve into cost and capacity, which meet at a budget.
> - **Property** — the quantities are relatable and comparable against a declared envelope, so demand can
>   be read against a bound rather than watched call by call.
> - **Quality attribute** — cost control, capacity planning, graceful behavior under load.

<!-- point: a-measurement-model-is-the-interpretation-placed-over-raw-events | The measurement model is the engineering interpretation placed over concrete events — requests, tokens, leases, charges — not the events themselves. -->
[ref:genai-cost-capacity-model] draws the model: generation work fans into usage and concurrency, which
lead to cost and capacity, which meet at a budget. It supports questions such as *how much capacity is
this workload consuming? what does a unit of work cost? how much work is currently active? how close is
current demand to an operating or budget envelope?* These quantities come from concrete events —
requests, token counts, leases, provider responses, and charges — but the measurement model is the
engineering interpretation placed over them.

<!-- point: representing-a-threshold-does-not-determine-what-happens-when-it-is-crossed | Representing a quantity and a bound is separate from deciding the consequence of crossing it; the same measurements support observation, throttling, soft completion, or a dormant hard gate. -->
The distinction matters because representing a threshold does not determine what happens when the
threshold is crossed. In DocAble the same broad family of cost and usage measurements supports several
kinds of response. Some quantities are observed. Some feed adaptive throttling. Per-job exhaustion can
lead to soft completion rather than outright failure. A hard daily budget gate also exists, but stays
dormant unless configured. Those are different authority choices over related measurements.

For now, Part II needs only the representation. [ref:measured-quantity-comparison] reduces it to its
core shape: a measured quantity read as a current state against a reference envelope, and the comparison
between them.

Part III returns to this same example and asks the harder question: *when should a measured quantity
merely inform, when should it adapt behavior, and when should it stop work?* [ref:modeling-perp-authority]
holds the two moves apart — the Part II model of what quantities matter, and the Part III choice of what
consequence each quantity earns, from observation through adaptation and soft degradation to a hard gate
that may be armed or left dormant.
