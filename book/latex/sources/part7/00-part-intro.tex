A software engineer who delegates implementation to agents does not stop being an engineer. But the work changes. Time once spent writing and debugging code can instead be spent deciding what a system must do, examining alternative designs, establishing evidence, and governing delegated work. Some of these activities are familiar; others become more important when implementation can proceed much faster than a person can inspect it.

The change also raises questions about engineering methods and professional expertise. Which established practices become more valuable, and which need reconsideration? Similar questions arise in other disciplines, where engineers have long separated design from realization but face different constraints on what can be delegated. The boundary of delegation is not simply a matter of what an agent can perform; it also depends on the evidence available and the authority required to accept the result.

\cref{chapter:7.1-reorganization-of-se} examines the reorganization of software work, and \cref{chapter:7.2-software-rejoins-engineering} places MAGE in relation to established engineering methods. \cref{chapter:7.3-agentic-engineering-beyond-software} considers its application beyond software. We then turn to the engineer's expertise, education, and authority (\cref{chapter:7.4-the-engineer}), before considering what people and institutions must retain to remain answerable for the resulting systems (\cref{chapter:7.5-what-cannot-be-delegated}).

\begin{quote}
\mbox{}%
\subsection{Key Ideas}\label{heading:the-synthesis}

As agents perform more implementation work, engineers must reconsider where their expertise is most valuable. Their responsibility for the resulting systems remains, even as the work they perform directly changes.

These questions extend beyond software. Other engineering disciplines already rely on models, evidence, and delegated realization, but differ in what can be represented, checked, and controlled. MAGE offers a way to examine how autonomous agents might fit into those practices.
