*How do I run the method — and what is it all for?*

Orient to the healthy state first, then route a break to its class. Run the fleet as cattle, not pets.
Automate by cost times frequency, and leave the cheap-and-rare failure to judgment rather than build the first
brick of a teetering tower. Bank your context so a fresh session rebuilds it. Do all this and the payoff is
the book's whole argument: a governed environment produces trustworthy software, because the discipline lives
in the environment, not in anyone's head.

This is the doctrine card — the deck's frame, not another gauge. It carries three stances for running the
method and closes on the synthesis every other card serves.

### Three stances

- **Running many agents.** Operate the herd with runbooks, gates, and controls — cattle, not pets. Agents
  stay at or under the pool cap, each has a live record, landed work is cleaned up. A misbehaving agent is
  replaced, not coaxed. Reads the manage-agents lifecycle.
- **When not to automate.** Govern by cost times frequency. The cheap-and-rare failure is left to judgment; a
  mechanism there is the first brick in a tower whose upkeep becomes the work itself. Price a control's upkeep
  before you build it. Reads the Sizing Matrix.
- **Operating near context limits.** Bank in-flight state so a fresh session reconstructs it; compaction is
  safe when the state is saved, and idle capacity with queued work is its own signal. Reads the
  manage-context lifecycle.

The doctrine turns on two axes — failure cost and failure frequency — the Sizing Matrix's product. Reach
grows with cost times frequency.

### The closing frame

The Modeling Thesis supplies the map; the Alignment Thesis holds it true; together they yield the governed
engineering environment — fast, forgetful, capable machines producing trustworthy software because the
discipline lives in the environment, not in anyone's head. Every other card in the deck is an instrument
reading of that one claim.
