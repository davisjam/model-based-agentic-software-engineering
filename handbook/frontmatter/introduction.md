This handbook is organized around distinct engineering judgments, not a lifecycle. The chapters
move from the foundations of engineering work — the software medium, process, teamwork, and
engineering knowledge — to requirements, specification, architecture, design, validation, and
research and development, because separating these judgments makes each one easier to understand.
Real engineering does not proceed in that order. The decisions overlap and interact, and engineers
revisit them as they build the machine and learn from it: a prototype may change a requirement,
and a design may expose an architectural gap. The organization is therefore conceptual rather than
procedural — each chapter isolates one kind of judgment, and all of them concern a machine that
participates in the world (@fig-judgment-machine-world).
