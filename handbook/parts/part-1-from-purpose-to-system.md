Software begins with a purpose in the world, but purpose alone does not tell us what to build.
Engineers must discover what matters, decide what they can responsibly promise, determine which
realizations would satisfy those promises, organize one such realization, resolve the choices inside
its parts, and gather evidence that the result is acceptable. Part I examines these engineering
problems in turn, along with the processes and teamwork needed to solve them. The order is conceptual
rather than procedural: real projects move among these problems as new information makes earlier
decisions worth revisiting.
