A system is not acceptable merely because it performs the right functions. It may also need to remain
available when components fail, respond within a deadline, resist unauthorized access, reveal enough of
its internal state to be operated, and remain understandable enough to change safely. These properties
are sometimes grouped as non-functional requirements, but they do not form a separate stage of
engineering. Each can shape requirements, specification, architecture, design, implementation,
validation, deployment, and evolution. Part II therefore turns the view from engineering activities to
the properties that cut across them. The question is no longer only how we move from purpose to system,
but how we make an important property hold throughout that system and preserve it as the system changes.
