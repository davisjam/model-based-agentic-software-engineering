## 8. What would a careful reader be wrong to believe?

Reading this product's commit log — thirty-odd landing messages, each accurate about its own wave —
a careful reader would come away believing four things that are not true:

**1. That "UX-I1: 0 affordance gaps over 24 capabilities" means the workbench can do everything
through both interfaces.** The parity claim is real and this audit reproduced it — but the registry
is the boundary of the claim. Machines, variables, quantities, events, domains and relation types
are semantic constructs the kernel supports and neither interface can author, and they are invisible
to UX-I1 because nobody registered them as capabilities. The registry was designed with an `absent`
status precisely so incomplete work would be named; the capabilities that would be `absent` are the
ones never declared. Parity holds; completeness is narrower than the number suggests.

**2. That the gates at HEAD verify everything the commit messages verified.** Mostly they do —
that is this codebase's genuine distinction — but the edges drift: the Worker-spawn half of a
landed claim has no gate (DEFECT-4), the bundle figures are one wave stale (DEFECT-5), and the
full-site build is not reproducible from a clean checkout without an undocumented asset-regen step.
Each wave's numbers were measured at that wave; nothing re-stamps them.

**3. That the requirement documents describe the shipped product.** They are conversation
transcripts, and later rulings reversed parts of them without amending them: expected values were
"accepted for v0.1" and then ruled out; Comunica was "what I would start with" and was measured and
rejected; SMT, view operations, reload persistence, and Linked mode are in the documents and not in
the product. The rulings are recorded — in DESIGN/DECISIONS files and SEMANTICS ⚠️ blocks — but the
requirements text itself was never reconciled, so requirements-and-nothing-else is the single most
misleading read of this repository.

**4. That the product's own descriptions of itself are current.** The shipped page says two
examples exist and Document Processing is unbuilt, beside a dropdown offering three (DEFECT-1).
PLAN.md's known-gaps list names six gaps, all closed (DEFECT-2). Both err in opposite directions
from the commit log: the log oversells nothing this audit found, while the standing documents
undersell a product that outran them. The gap between what was shipped and what was said is, here,
mostly the gap between a self-correcting commit record and self-describing artifacts with no gate
to keep them honest — the next wave of controls belongs on the prose, not the code.
